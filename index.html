The Craft

I still believe in craft. It’s the language only a human being can write. A distinct POV, a signature turn of phrase. Complex ideas made simple, compelling, even beautiful.

The writing a brand is judged on

Launch copy, founder letters, campaigns, rebrands. Big spotlight moments that carry the most weight and the least room for error.

Storytelling, not just messaging

For brands selling more than a product: an experience, a world, a way of life. Written with a storyteller's instinct, not a marketer's.

All mediums with one standard

Email, web, product, events. Whatever the format, the same question applies: does this need real craft, or just words? If it's the former, I'm in.

The Rules

Rules make craft repeatable, a thousand times over. The same human judgement, made explicit enough for a machine to check, so it survives contact with scale.

Tone of voice for humans, AI, or both

Written for people, the way it's always been done. Or rewritten as rules for an agent. Both let you control your voice, across teams and touchpoints.

UX writing rules for your UI, from foundations to components. The rules let designers and AI tools keep your product consistent as it grows.

Accuracy and maintenance

Voice rules go stale. Models change, new features ship, teams turn over. I offer ongoing maintenance to keep your AI content systems up to date.

Rules in action:

Fin (Intercom)

Senior Content Designer

I wrote the company's first content design guidelines. These included foundational rules for the whole product UI – and specific rules for every component in the design system. Then I built three tools on top, all shipped and in production:

1

VERBI — Creation

An AI assistant built on Glean that writes, edits, and critiques copy in the house style. It means judgment scaled across an entire team rather than bottlenecking with one person. Read my Intercom blog post →

2

content-review — Validation

A Claude Code skill that checks whatever a designer gives it: code, a Figma file, a screenshot. The content is reviewed against the full design system, so any issues are caught while the work is in progress, not fixed after launch.

3

content-lint — Enforcement

Code gets linted automatically, so I built this tool to ensure content does too. It checks every pull request for major errors like typos or US vs UK English. It found 5k errors live in-product, and now catches new ones before they ship.

Example: a content guideline, written for AI to check

To show what this means in practice, here's an illustrative example.

Written for humans in a style guide:

Buttons should use short, active verbs. Avoid vague labels like "Submit" or "OK". Say what the button actually does e.g. "Send message," "Save changes". Keep to 3 words or fewer where possible.

The same rule, restructured for a machine to follow:

rule: button_label_specificity
applies_to: component.button
checks:
  - max_words: 3
  - banned_labels: ["Submit", "OK", "Click here"]
  - require: verb_first
  - require: describes_the_action  # e.g. "Send message" not "Continue"
fail_message: >
  Button labels should be short, active, and specific to the action
  (e.g. "Send message", not "Submit").

The human version relies on judgment: what counts as "vague" is left to taste.

The machine version can’t do this: every condition has to be explicit and checkable, with no room for interpretation. Writing that translation – deciding what can be made binary and what still needs a person’s judgment – was the core challenge behind all three tools I built above.
